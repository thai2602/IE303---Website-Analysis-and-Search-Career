# CV Red Flags: Warning Signs to Watch for During Screening

## Metadata
- **Topic**: CV / Resume Red Flags and Risk Signals
- **Audience**: HR Professionals, Recruiters
- **Language**: English

---

## Overview

Identifying red flags during CV screening helps HR quickly spot candidates who may pose a risk to the organization. These warning signs do not automatically disqualify a candidate, but each warrants careful consideration before moving forward.

> **Key principle**: Red flags are signals to investigate further — not automatic rejections. Context and explanation matter.

---

## 1. Employment Gaps

**Description**: Unexplained periods of unemployment or inactivity in a candidate's work history.

**Why it matters**: Gaps without reasonable explanation may indicate instability, performance issues, or gaps in professional development.

**How to handle**:
- Ask the candidate to explain the gap during screening or interview
- Where possible, seek clarification from previous employers
- Accept valid reasons: caregiving responsibilities, health recovery, further education, relocation

---

## 2. Frequent Job-Hopping

**Description**: A pattern of changing companies or roles every 1–2 years or less.

**Why it matters**: High turnover frequency may reflect lack of long-term commitment, difficulty adapting, or impatience with work environments.

**How to handle**:
- Investigate the reasons behind each transition — via interview or cover letter
- Accept valid reasons: career advancement, toxic environments, company closure, industry shifts
- Distinguish between intentional moves (promotions, growth) and reactive departures

---

## 3. Lack of Personalization

**Description**: A generic CV that has not been tailored to the specific role or company.

**Why it matters**: Sending identical CVs to multiple employers without adjusting for the JD suggests the candidate has not researched the role and is not genuinely invested in the opportunity.

**How to handle**:
- Prioritize CVs that reference role-specific skills, company values, or JD language
- A personalized CV signals that the candidate understands the position and has put in genuine effort

---

## 4. Spelling, Grammar & Factual Errors

**Description**: Typos, grammatical mistakes, or inconsistent information within the CV.

**Why it matters**: Errors signal a lack of attention to detail and professionalism — qualities that matter in virtually every role.

**How to handle**:
- Minor isolated errors: allow the candidate to clarify during the interview
- Repeated or serious errors: treat as a signal of low care and responsibility
- Pay special attention to errors in key sections such as job titles, dates, and contact information

---

## 5. Vague or Unsubstantiated Descriptions

**Description**: Job responsibilities and achievements described in overly general terms, without specific outcomes or metrics.

**Examples**:

| Vague (Red Flag) | Specific (Green Flag) |
|------------------|----------------------|
| "Contributed to company growth" | "Grew revenue by 20% over 6 months" |
| "Worked on marketing campaigns" | "Managed $10K ad budget, achieving 3x ROAS" |
| "Helped improve team efficiency" | "Reduced report turnaround time by 30% through process redesign" |

**Why it matters**: Vague descriptions make it impossible to assess actual impact or capability.

**How to handle**:
- Ask candidates to clarify specific achievements and their direct role in key projects
- Request quantified evidence where relevant, especially for senior roles

---

## 6. Inconsistency with External Profiles (LinkedIn, Portfolio)

**Description**: Information in the CV does not match the candidate's LinkedIn profile, portfolio, or other public records — such as differing job titles, dates, or educational background.

**Why it matters**: Discrepancies may indicate dishonesty, deliberate omission of unfavorable information, or simple carelessness.

**How to handle**:
- Cross-check CV against LinkedIn, portfolio sites, and any publicly available information
- Flag any significant inconsistencies and request clarification during the interview
- Pay particular attention to mismatched employment dates or role titles

---

## 7. Missing Key Skills or Achievements

**Description**: The CV omits critical competencies, tools, or notable results expected for the role.

**Why it matters**: A candidate who fails to highlight core qualifications may lack the experience — or may not have invested enough effort in presenting themselves effectively.

**How to handle**:
- Ask the candidate to provide additional detail on relevant skills and projects
- Determine whether the omission reflects a genuine gap or simply poor CV presentation
- Use the CV scorecard to objectively assess what is and isn't present

---

## Summary: Red Flags Quick Reference

| # | Red Flag | Primary Risk | First Action |
|---|----------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | Employment gaps | Instability or performance issues | Request explanation |
| 2 | Frequent job-hopping | Lack of commitment | Probe reasons in interview |
| 3 | No personalization | Low genuine interest | Deprioritize in shortlist |
| 4 | Spelling / grammar errors | Poor attention to detail | Assess severity and frequency |
| 5 | Vague descriptions | Unverifiable capability | Ask for specific examples |
| 6 | Inconsistency with LinkedIn | Potential dishonesty | Cross-check and clarify |
| 7 | Missing key skills / achievements | Incomplete or weak profile | Request supplementary detail |

---

## Key Takeaway

Red flags are investigation triggers, not automatic disqualifiers. HR should identify, document, and follow up on each signal — using structured interviews and reference checks to build a complete, accurate picture of the candidate before making a final decision.
