## Overview

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software used by companies to automatically collect, parse, and rank CVs before a human recruiter ever sees them. A candidate may be highly qualified but still get filtered out if their CV isn't structured for ATS compatibility.

This guide helps HR professionals understand how ATS works — and how to advise candidates to optimize their CVs accordingly.

---

## 1. How ATS Works

### 1.1 The Parsing Process
When a candidate submits a CV, the ATS:
1. **Extracts** text from the document (name, contact info, work history, skills, education)
2. **Parses** the content into structured fields
3. **Scores** the CV based on keyword matches against the Job Description (JD)
4. **Ranks** candidates and surfaces the highest-scoring profiles to the recruiter

### 1.2 What ATS Evaluates
| ATS Signal | What It Looks For |
|------------|-------------------|
| Keywords | Exact or near-exact matches with JD terms |
| Job titles | Alignment with the role being advertised |
| Section headers | Standard labels (e.g. "Work Experience", "Education") |
| Date formats | Consistent, machine-readable formats (e.g. MM/YYYY) |
| File format | Clean text extraction — typically prefers `.docx` or plain `.pdf` |

### 1.3 Why Candidates Fail ATS Screening
- CV is submitted as an image-based PDF (text cannot be extracted)
- Keywords don't match the JD language
- Non-standard section headers confuse the parser
- Formatting elements (tables, columns, graphics) break text extraction

## 2. ATS-Compliant CV Formatting

### 2.1 Recommended File Format
| Format | ATS Compatibility | Notes |
|--------|------------------|-------|
| `.docx` (Word) | ✅ Best | Most universally parsed |
| Text-based `.pdf` | ✅ Good | Must be text-selectable, not scanned |
| Image-based `.pdf` | ❌ Avoid | Text cannot be extracted |
| `.pages`, `.odt` | ⚠️ Risky | May not be supported by all ATS platforms |

### 2.2 Layout & Structure Rules

**Use standard section headers** — ATS parsers recognize common labels:
- `Work Experience` / `Professional Experience`
- `Education`
- `Skills`
- `Certifications`
- `Summary` / `Professional Summary`

**Avoid** creative or ambiguous headers like:
- "My Journey", "What I've Done", "Where I've Been"

**Use a single-column layout**:
- Multi-column layouts cause ATS to misread the reading order
- Avoid tables for structuring key content — use plain bullet points instead

**Font and spacing**:
- Use standard fonts: Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, or Georgia
- Font size: 10–12pt for body text, 14–16pt for name/headers
- Avoid text boxes, headers/footers (ATS often skips these), and icons

### 2.3 Recommended CV Structure for ATS

```
1. Full Name & Contact Information
2. Professional Summary (3–5 lines)
3. Core Skills / Key Competencies
4. Work Experience (reverse chronological)
5. Education
6. Certifications & Professional Development
7. Optional: Projects, Volunteer Work, Publications
```

## 3. Keyword Optimization by JD

### 3.1 Why Keywords Matter
ATS ranks CVs based on how closely the language matches the JD. A candidate who uses different terminology for the same skill may be filtered out despite being fully qualified.

**Example**:
- JD says: `"Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"`
- Candidate writes: `"Organic traffic growth"` → May not match
- Better: `"Search Engine Optimization (SEO) — grew organic traffic by 45%"`

### 3.2 How to Extract Keywords from a JD

Advise candidates to scan the JD for:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| **Hard skills / tools** | Python, Salesforce, Google Analytics, SQL |
| **Soft skills** | Project management, cross-functional collaboration |
| **Certifications** | PMP, ACCA, AWS Certified, Google Ads |
| **Job title variants** | "Marketing Manager" vs. "Marketing Lead" |
| **Industry terms** | KPI, SLA, B2B, SaaS, CAC, ROAS |

### 3.3 Keyword Placement Strategy

| CV Section | Keyword Usage |
|------------|--------------|
| Professional Summary | Include 3–5 high-priority keywords naturally |
| Core Skills section | List exact-match keywords from the JD |
| Work Experience bullets | Embed keywords in context with measurable results |
| Certifications | Use full official names as they appear in the JD |

> **Important for HR**: Remind candidates that keyword stuffing (listing terms without context) can pass ATS but will fail human review. Keywords must appear naturally within genuine experience descriptions.

### 3.4 Use Both Acronyms and Full Forms
ATS may search for one or the other. Candidates should include both:
- `Search Engine Optimization (SEO)`
- `Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)`
- `Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)`

### 3.5 Writing High-Impact Work Experience Bullets (STAR & Google XYZ)

To impress both the ATS parser and the human recruiter, candidates should structure their work experience bullet points using professional frameworks:
- **STAR Method**:
  - **Situation**: Context of the challenge or problem.
  - **Task**: The responsibility, role, or goal.
  - **Action**: The specific steps taken (starting with strong Action Verbs).
  - **Result**: Quantifiable outcomes, metrics, and achievements.
  - *Vietnamese translation / ứng dụng*: Phần kinh nghiệm làm việc nên được viết bằng cách bắt đầu mỗi gạch đầu dòng (bullet point) bằng một động từ hành động mạnh (như: Triển khai, Tối ưu, Dẫn dắt, Xây dựng). Đồng thời áp dụng cấu trúc **STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result)** để trình bày rõ ràng, chi tiết về bối cảnh, nhiệm vụ, hành động và đưa ra các số liệu cụ thể nhằm chứng minh kết quả/tác động thực tế.
- **Google's XYZ Formula**:
  - **Formula**: *"Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]"*
  - **Application**: Start each bullet point with a strong action verb (e.g., *Spearheaded, Implemented, Optimized, Led, Built, Engineered, Deployed, Developed, Automated, Designed*) followed by the quantifiable metric (percentage, number, duration) and the specific action taken.
  - **Example**: *"Implemented an automated regression testing suite using Selenium and Appium, reducing testing time by 75% (from 8 hours to 2 hours) and expanding test coverage by 40%."*
  - *Vietnamese translation / ứng dụng*: Công thức XYZ của Google: **"Hoàn thành [X], đo lường bằng [Y], thông qua thực hiện [Z]"** để đưa ra số liệu cụ thể và chứng minh tác động thực tế.
    - Ví dụ: *"Triển khai hệ thống tự động hóa kiểm thử regression sử dụng Selenium và Appium, giảm thời gian kiểm thử đi 75% (từ 8 giờ xuống 2 giờ) và mở rộng độ bao phủ kiểm thử thêm 40%."*

## 4. Common ATS Mistakes to Avoid

### 4.1 Formatting Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's a Problem | Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----|
| Using tables for layout | ATS reads table cells out of order | Use plain bullet lists |
| Text in headers/footers | Many ATS platforms skip this area | Move all key info into the body |
| Embedded images or logos | Cannot be parsed as text | Remove all non-text elements |
| Multi-column layout | Causes jumbled reading order | Switch to single-column |
| Fancy fonts or icons | May not render correctly in ATS | Use standard system fonts only |
| Self-rating skills with progress bars or pie charts | Cannot be parsed as text by ATS, hides actual text keywords, and appears highly unprofessional to recruiters | List skills as simple text lists with standard labels |
| Overly colorful layouts with shapes and text boxes | Confuses the ATS parser reading order and prevents clean text extraction | Use simple black-and-white, single-column plain text layouts |

> **Vietnamese translation / Giải thích chi tiết**: Vì phần lớn các hệ thống quản lý ứng viên (ATS) thế hệ cũ hoặc định dạng parser tiêu chuẩn không thể phân tích chính xác cấu trúc bảng biểu, biểu đồ hình ảnh hay sơ đồ. Các thông tin nằm trong bảng hoặc hộp văn bản (text box) thường bị đọc lộn xộn hoặc biến mất hoàn toàn, dẫn đến việc hồ sơ bị loại tự động do thiếu từ khóa.

### 4.2 Content Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's a Problem | Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----|
| Generic job titles | Doesn't match JD role labels | Mirror the exact job title from the JD |
| Vague skill descriptions | No keyword match | Use specific tool/skill names |
| Missing dates | ATS can't calculate experience duration | Always include MM/YYYY for all roles |
| Unexplained gaps | May trigger low scoring | Add a brief note (e.g. "Career break — professional development") |
| Submitting one CV for all roles | Low keyword match per JD | Tailor each CV to the specific JD |

### 4.3 File Submission Mistakes
- Sending a scanned PDF or photo of a CV
- Submitting a password-protected file
- Using a file name with special characters (e.g. `CV_2024@final!!.pdf`) — use clean names like `FirstName_LastName_CV.pdf`

**Keywords**
- [ ] JD reviewed and key terms identified
- [ ] High-priority keywords included in Summary and Skills sections
- [ ] Both acronyms and full forms used where relevant
- [ ] Keywords embedded naturally within experience descriptions

**Content**
- [ ] All roles include start and end dates (MM/YYYY)

- [ ] Employment gaps are briefly addressed

**File**

- [ ] File is not password-protected
- [ ] CV is not a scanned image

## 6. The Evolution of ATS: Legacy vs. AI-Powered

Modern ATS platforms are integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) and semantic search, shifting from rigid keyword matching to contextual understanding. This evolution changes how CVs should be optimized.

| Feature | Legacy ATS (Keyword-Based) | AI-Powered ATS (Semantic-Based) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Parsing Mechanism** | Exact keyword matching (e.g., requires "Node.js" exactly as written). | Understands context and synonyms (e.g., groups "Node.js", "Express", and "MongoDB" under "Backend Development"). |
| **Scoring Logic** | Frequency-based (counts how many times a keyword appears). | Context-based (evaluates the depth of experience and how recently a skill was used). |
| **Optimization Strategy** | Rigidly mirroring the JD word-for-word. | Telling a cohesive professional story; focusing on the application of technical and soft skills. |

## 7. Debunking Common ATS Myths

Many candidates follow outdated or incorrect advice when formatting their CVs. HR should actively dispel these myths to ensure high-quality applications.

- **The "White Text" Hack:** Hiding keywords in white text to boost ATS scores is a highly penalized practice. When the ATS extracts text, all formatting is stripped. Human recruiters will immediately see a block of incoherent keywords and reject the application.
- **"PDF is Always Better":** While PDFs preserve visual formatting, some older ATS platforms struggle to parse them. If the application portal explicitly requests a `.docx` file, candidates must follow that instruction.
- **Keyword Stuffing:** Dumping a list of technologies or acronyms at the bottom of the page might trigger a high automated score, but it fails the human review stage. Skills must be demonstrated in context.

## 8. Content Frameworks & Actionable Writing

ATS parsers and human recruiters alike look for impact. Candidates should structure their bullet points using standardized frameworks like the **STAR Method** (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

### 8.1 Use Strong Action Verbs
Start every bullet point with a high-impact verb rather than passive phrases like "Responsible for" or "Tasked with".
- *Technical roles:* Architected, Engineered, Deployed, Integrated, Optimized.
- *Leadership/Business roles:* Spearheaded, Orchestrated, Facilitated, Mentored, Grew.

### 8.2 Quantify the Impact (ATS Contextual Scoring)
ATS algorithms are designed to recognize numbers, percentages, and timeframes as indicators of proficiency.

- **Weak:** "Added AI features to a web application."
- **Strong:** "Integrated local LLMs into a Next.js platform using LangChain, improving automated response accuracy by 35%."
- **Weak:** "Did SEO for a university review website."
- **Strong:** "Executed entity-based SEO strategies and resolved Google Search Console indexing bottlenecks, increasing organic traffic by 45%."

## 9. Handling External Links & Portfolios

For roles like Full-stack Engineers or UI/UX Designers, external portfolios (GitHub repositories, Figma prototypes, live web applications) are critical.

### 9.1 Link Formatting Rules
- **Write out the full URL:** Do not use hyperlinked text like `[Click here for my GitHub]`. During text extraction, the underlying URL is often stripped, leaving the recruiter with a dead end.
- **Keep it clean:** Write clean paths like `github.com/username/project` or `linkedin.com/in/username`. Remove long tracking parameters (e.g., UTM tags).
- **Placement:** Place all critical links in the Contact Information section at the very top of the CV so they are parsed into the primary candidate profile fields.

### 9.2 Does ATS Actually Crawl External Links?

This is a common misconception HR should address clearly. In almost all cases, **ATS platforms do not crawl or index external URLs**. The link is stored as a plain text string in the candidate profile. The practical implications are:

- The ATS will not score a candidate higher based on the content of their GitHub or portfolio.
- The link serves a purely human purpose — a recruiter clicks it manually during the review stage.
- Therefore, the CV text itself must stand alone in passing the ATS scoring threshold. The portfolio only supplements the human review.

**Advice for candidates:** Never rely on a portfolio link to compensate for missing keywords or thin work experience descriptions in the CV body.

## 10. Multilingual CVs & Cross-Border Applications

When applying for roles in international markets, specific technical considerations must be made regarding document encoding and phrasing.

### 10.1 Character Encoding & Fonts
If applying for roles requiring non-Latin characters (e.g., targeting a position in Japan), the CV must use standard embedded fonts.
- **The Risk of Mojibake:** If a candidate uses custom fonts for Kanji or Hiragana and exports to PDF without proper embedding, the ATS will parse the text as corrupted, unreadable symbols (Mojibake).
- **Solution:** Stick to universally supported unicode fonts like MS Mincho, Arial Unicode MS, or Noto Sans.

### 10.2 Language Consistency
Do not mix languages within the same description. If the JD is in English, the entire CV should be in English. Certifications (e.g., JLPT N3) should be listed by their official English title alongside the acronym: `"Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (JLPT) - N3"`

### 10.3 Southeast Asian Market Considerations

HR professionals operating in Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, and the Philippines face unique multilingual challenges that Section 10.1 does not fully address.

| Scenario | Risk | Recommended Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnamese CV with diacritics (ắ, ổ, ề) submitted to a foreign ATS | Diacritics may parse as garbled characters if encoding is not UTF-8 | Save `.docx` files with UTF-8 encoding; verify PDF text-selection before submitting |
| Bilingual CV (English + Vietnamese) for a regional role | ATS keyword scoring only processes the primary language; bilingual sections dilute match scores | Submit a fully English CV; list local certifications in English with Vietnamese name in parentheses |
| Thai script in a CV targeting an international ATS | Thai characters require specific font embedding; many ATS platforms default to Latin-only parsing | For international applications, use English throughout; for local Thai companies, confirm ATS support before submitting |
| Chinese-language CVs (Traditional/Simplified) for cross-border roles | ATS may not distinguish between Traditional and Simplified; OCR failure risk on scanned documents | Always submit text-based PDFs; use a dual-language header only if the JD explicitly requests it |

> **Key Principle for HR in SEA markets:** When in doubt, the safest approach is a fully English CV for any role posted in English, regardless of the candidate's nationality or location. Local-language CVs should only be submitted when the JD is written in that local language.

## 11. Platform-Specific ATS Behavior

One of the most significant gaps in generic ATS advice is the assumption that all ATS platforms behave identically. In practice, the five most widely deployed platforms each have distinct parsing behaviors, scoring logic, and file format preferences. HR professionals should understand these differences when advising candidates or configuring job postings.

### 11.1 Overview of Major ATS Platforms

| Platform | Market Segment | Parsing Strength | Known Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Workday** | Enterprise (large corps) | Strong on `.docx`; good semantic scoring | Struggles with complex PDF layouts; multi-column CVs frequently misparse |
| **Greenhouse** | Mid-market / Tech startups | Excellent text extraction; supports `.pdf` and `.docx` equally well | Relies heavily on structured sections; non-standard headers reduce match accuracy |
| **Lever** | Mid-market / Growth companies | Good keyword matching; integrates with LinkedIn import | Limited handling of graphics-heavy CVs; image-based PDFs will fail entirely |
| **iCIMS** | Enterprise / High-volume hiring | Robust parsing across formats; handles tables better than most | Older scoring engine; may favor keyword frequency over contextual depth |
| **SAP SuccessFactors** | Large enterprise / Global corps | Supports multilingual parsing in configured regions | Configuration-dependent — behavior varies significantly by company setup; candidates cannot predict scoring logic |

### 11.2 Platform-Specific Optimization Tips

**Workday**
- Always submit `.docx` unless `.pdf` is explicitly specified in the application portal.
- Avoid multi-column layouts entirely — Workday's parser reads left-to-right across the full page width, causing columns to merge.
- The "Easy Apply" via LinkedIn integration bypasses CV parsing; candidates should still upload a tailored CV separately when the option is available.

**Greenhouse**
- Section headers are critical. Greenhouse maps content to predefined fields (Work Experience, Education, Skills). Non-standard headers such as "What I've Built" will cause content to be dropped into an unstructured "Other" field.
- Greenhouse supports cover letter parsing. Advise candidates to include 2–3 high-priority keywords in the cover letter as well.

**Lever**
- Lever integrates tightly with LinkedIn profiles. If a candidate applies via LinkedIn, their profile data — not their uploaded CV — is the primary parsed input. Candidates should ensure their LinkedIn profile is also keyword-optimized for the target role.
- For direct CV uploads, `.pdf` performs reliably. Text-based PDFs with clean single-column layouts parse accurately.

**iCIMS**
- iCIMS uses an older keyword-frequency scoring engine, meaning keyword repetition across sections (Summary, Skills, Experience) still materially improves scores.
- iCIMS has better-than-average support for tables in the Skills section specifically — a bulleted skills table is acceptable here, unlike other platforms.

**SAP SuccessFactors**
- This platform is heavily configured by the employer. Two companies using SuccessFactors may have entirely different scoring thresholds, required fields, and keyword weights.
- Candidates cannot reliably reverse-engineer SuccessFactors scoring. The best strategy is a clean, standard CV with strong keyword alignment to the JD — this maximizes compatibility regardless of configuration.

### 11.3 How to Identify Which ATS a Company Uses

Candidates can often identify the ATS before applying, which allows them to tailor formatting accordingly. Common detection methods:

- **Check the application URL:** Workday URLs typically contain `myworkdayjobs.com`; Greenhouse uses `boards.greenhouse.io`; Lever uses `jobs.lever.co`; iCIMS uses `icims.com` in the portal URL.
- **Check the job posting source:** Roles posted on the company's own career page often embed the ATS portal within an iframe — hovering over the "Apply" button reveals the destination URL.
- **Use tools like Jobscan or RezScore:** These tools identify ATS platforms and simulate scoring for the candidate's CV against a specific JD.

> **HR Note:** Sharing this detection method with candidates is valuable coaching. A candidate who knows they're applying via Greenhouse will format their section headers correctly, reducing the rate of technically qualified candidates being filtered out incorrectly.

## 12. ATS Scoring Thresholds & Match Rate

Understanding how ATS scoring thresholds work is essential for HR professionals who want to calibrate job postings and advise candidates effectively.

### 12.1 How Scoring Works

Most ATS platforms assign a **match score** (typically expressed as a percentage or a ranked tier) to each application. This score is calculated by comparing the CV content against the JD using one of two methods:

- **Keyword frequency scoring (legacy):** Counts how many times JD keywords appear in the CV. Higher frequency = higher score.
- **Semantic/contextual scoring (modern):** Evaluates whether the candidate demonstrates proficiency in the required areas, even if exact wording differs.

### 12.2 Typical Scoring Thresholds

While thresholds vary by company configuration, industry benchmarks suggest:

| Score Range | Typical Outcome |
|---|---|
| 80–100% | Prioritized for human review; often fast-tracked to phone screen |
| 60–79% | Enters the recruiter queue; reviewed when higher-scoring pool is thin |
| 40–59% | Deprioritized; reviewed only if volume is low |
| Below 40% | Automatically filtered out in most configurations |

> **Important:** These thresholds are configurable. A company with a high applicant volume may set the auto-reject threshold at 60%. A company struggling to fill a niche technical role may lower it to 30%. HR teams should review and adjust their ATS thresholds regularly to avoid over-filtering qualified candidates.

### 12.3 How to Advise Candidates on Improving Match Rate

1. **Run a gap analysis:** Have the candidate paste their CV and the JD into a free tool like Jobscan. The tool identifies missing keywords and calculates an estimated match score before submission.
2. **Prioritize the top 10 keywords:** Not all keywords are weighted equally. Job titles, required certifications, and skills listed under "Requirements" (vs. "Nice to Have") carry the highest weight.
3. **Mirror the JD language precisely:** If the JD says "stakeholder management," the CV should say "stakeholder management" — not "stakeholder engagement" or "managing stakeholders."
4. **Target 70%+ match before submitting:** As a general coaching benchmark, advise candidates not to submit until their estimated match rate exceeds 70% on the priority keywords.

### 12.4 HR-Side Calibration: Avoiding Over-Filtering

ATS scoring is only as good as the JD it scores against. Poorly written JDs produce poor screening results. HR professionals should:

- **Avoid JD keyword inflation:** Listing 25 required skills dilutes scoring accuracy. Limit hard requirements to 8–12 genuinely essential skills.
- **Use standard industry terminology in JDs:** If the market uses "Customer Success Manager" but your JD says "Client Happiness Lead," qualified candidates will score low because their CVs use the industry-standard term.
- **Review rejected candidate pools periodically:** Sample 10–15 auto-rejected CVs per quarter to check whether the ATS is filtering out qualified candidates due to terminology mismatches rather than genuine lack of experience.
